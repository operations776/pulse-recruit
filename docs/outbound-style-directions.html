Pulse / pillar 3 / outbound

Same structure.
Three styles.

One campaign screen, rendered three ways. The markup is identical in all three, cloned from a single template at the bottom of this file. Only the design tokens change: colour, type, radius, depth, density. That is the whole argument for restyling rather than rebuilding.

Two things in here do not exist in Pulse today and should, whichever direction wins: the dispatch ribbon across the top, which shows what actually sends in the next hour per mailbox, and the step chain, which reads as one connected sequence instead of a stack of unrelated boxes.

Direction 01

Switchboard

Outbound is a switchboard operator's desk: you are patching people through to each other, and the job is knowing which lines are live. Cool grey-blue panel, cobalt as the only action colour, condensed display face, tight 5px radii. Steps are wired together with a visible patch cable and a jack port, so a sequence looks like a circuit rather than a list.

Densest of the three and the most obviously an instrument. Best if you want Pulse to feel like professional equipment next to Instantly's soft SaaS look.

Palette
Type
Saira Condensed / Inter / JetBrains Mono
Signature
Patch cable and jack port connecting the steps
Risk
Cobalt on grey can read corporate if the condensed face is dropped
01 switchboard

Direction 02

Night desk

Built for the person who has this open for nine hours. Deep pine-slate rather than the usual near-black, so it does not read as a developer tool, with two accents doing different jobs: amber is the verb, every clickable thing, and mint is alive, every send that is queued and moving. Reuses the IBM Plex family Pulse already loads, so adopting it costs nothing in font weight.

The kindest on the eyes and the one that makes the dispatch ribbon sing, because ticks of light on a dark track read as traffic. Means committing to a dark app or building both themes.

Palette
Type
IBM Plex Sans Condensed / IBM Plex Sans / IBM Plex Mono
Signature
Live send traffic as lit ticks on a dark track
Risk
The ATS is light today, so this needs a theme system or a full flip
02 night desk

Direction 03

Pressroom

Keeps Archivo Black, the face Pulse already has, and pushes it much harder: oversized tabular metrics, uppercase heads, near-zero radius, and a single hot magenta doing all the work the vermilion used to do. Steps stop being cards and become one ruled block with shared edges, which is the rule DESIGN.md already sets for the pipeline board.

The cheapest to ship, because it is a token swap on a system you already built, and the most opinionated on the page. Magenta is the risk and the reason it is memorable.

Palette
Type
Archivo Black / Inter / JetBrains Mono
Signature
Oversized metrics and a ruled step block, no floating cards
Risk
Magenta is a commitment, and it fights any brand colour you add later
03 pressroom

What I would do

Night desk for outbound, and build the theme system while we are in there. Outbound is the screen people leave open all day watching sends land, and it is the one place where a dark surface is a functional choice rather than a taste one. The dark track also makes the dispatch ribbon legible at a glance, which is the whole point of adding it.

If you would rather not run two themes, Pressroom is the honest second choice. It reuses the face you already load, it is a pure token swap, and it is the fastest to ship.

Whichever you pick, the structure below does not change, so this decision does not block the data model or the send engine. Tell me the number and I will write it into the spec.
